A message from our founder

Abdul Samad Khan

Founder and Chairman

Markaz started with a frustration I could not let go of. Schools across Karachi wanted a proper sports programme and could not find anyone to run one from end to end. A coach here, a supplier there, someone else to organise the fixtures, and nobody accountable when it all came apart halfway through the year.

So we put the whole thing under one roof. Certified coaches and PE teachers, the equipment to go with them, the fixtures, the camps and the gradings. If something goes wrong now, there is one number to call, and it is ours.

A child who learns to lose well on a Tuesday evening handles a great deal more than sport by the time they are eighteen.

How it started, in his own words.

Abdul Samad Khan has been training, competing and coaching since 1991. The company is the shape that experience eventually took.

1991

A school hall in Class Two

I started martial arts at Charlotte Public School when I was in Class Two, around 1991. ITC used to come to the school to teach us, and one of the great masters who taught us was Great Master Shahid Ali Siddiqui Sahib. Alongside him, Muhammad Yusuf, Fahad Ali and Nadeem Shah all had a hand in introducing us to the sport.

I trained with Shahid Sir for several years after that, and later under other instructors including Hakiman and Rashid Qureshi Sahib. Those early years are the foundation everything else was built on.

The early years

Four days a week that nobody scheduled

My own journey began with kickboxing, though I took part in almost every sport that was put in front of us — badminton included. I was training for the National Games at the time, competing in whatever local competitions we could find, and slowly I started training with the other athletes as much as against them.

The club ran three days a week. I would go, train, and come home. But there were four more days in the week and I wanted all of them, so I found training partners among my friends. We repeated techniques, sparred, and pushed each other along. That is how martial arts stopped being something I did at the club and became something I simply did.

Coaching

What Special Olympics taught me

Later I moved into coaching, and became involved with Special Olympics Pakistan, working with athletes of different abilities. That work changed what I thought coaching was. It is not simply teaching a technique or preparing someone for a competition. Very often it is understanding a person's life — their family, their circumstances, and what they are carrying when they walk in.

One athlete I coached, a young girl named Alina, has stayed with me. Her circumstances were difficult, and for years she kept supporting and protecting her family through them. Any one of those things could have stopped her. She did not let them.

She kept training, and she kept going. Watching her, I understood that the greatest victories in sport are not always the medals. Sometimes the real victory is a person getting past the things that were built to hold them back.

Abdul Samad Khan

Working with athletes like her gave me a different view of the job. As a coach you are not only developing an athlete. Some days you are helping build a person's confidence, their courage, and their belief that they are capable.

Qualifications

From the mat to the staff room

I studied at Casper University, where I took international courses in Physical Education and completed my qualifications. From there my work moved towards sports management and physical education inside schools.

I served as Head of Sports at the PAF Chapter of The City School, and afterwards at Happy Home and the Liberty Education System. Those years taught me how a sports programme actually survives inside an institution — the budgets, the timetables, the competitions, and the students who need someone to notice them.

Today

Beyond the academy

I am President of the Sindh Pickleball Association, working on the growth of the sport across the province, and a founding member of the Pakistan Sports Education Foundation.

Much of what we do now sits outside coaching altogether — seminars, training sessions, camps, and support for institutions that need uniforms, qualified coaches or a programme built from scratch.

The idea

Why martial arts and sport belong together

Sport has never been only about competition for me. It is education, discipline, confidence, teamwork, and an opportunity handed to someone who might not otherwise get one.

That is the thread running through all of it — the martial arts, the coaching, the sports education, the management. The academy is the place where those come together: not just training, but games, seminars, camps, athlete development, and real partnerships with schools and sporting bodies.

The aim is an ecosystem, not a production line. It should turn out better, more confident, more capable people. The athletes follow from that.
